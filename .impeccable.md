## Design Context

> **Branch note.** This file describes **Finn** (`fintech` branch) — the advisor-facing
> product. It was previously `master`'s copy, written for Asymptote's researcher/developer
> audience, and described a Windows tray app for "solo researchers exploring their own
> notes and code." That was the wrong audience and the wrong product. Rewritten 2026-08-17.
> Same drift `CLAUDE.md` warns about; check the branch before trusting this file.

### Users

**Solo RIAs and small advisor shops (1–5 advisors)**, plus mid-market firms in evaluation.
Registered fiduciaries handling other people's retirement money, personally liable for the
advice they give, and examinable by the SEC on it.

Two contexts, and the design has to serve both:

- **The advisor alone at 9pm**, doing their own meeting prep because there is nobody to
  delegate it to. Tired, moving fast, wants the page to tell them what changed and what
  cannot wait. This is the real recurring use.
- **The advisor in front of a prospect or client**, screen shared. Everything visible is
  now a claim they are personally accountable for. Nothing may look speculative, decorative,
  or unexplained.

They are not developers. They are fluent in money, not in software, and they have been sold
a lot of bad software.

### Job To Be Done

**Close the client meeting loop**: prep the meeting, record it, write it up. Measured in
minutes saved on a Tuesday, and in *not being wrong in front of a client*.

The second half is the load-bearing one. A number this product shows wrong is a number an
advisor repeats out loud to a client. Every figure carries provenance, and a figure that
could not be computed says so instead of guessing. **The interface's primary job is to make
the difference between "verified" and "unavailable" instantly legible** — that distinction
is the product.

### Brand Personality

Three words: **Exacting. Discreet. Unhurried.**

- **Exacting** — every number traces to a source. The UI shows its work: which file, which
  column, which check. Precision is the feature, not a detail.
- **Discreet** — this handles client PII inside a regulated boundary. Discretion reads as
  restraint: no exclamation, no celebration, no gamification, nothing that would embarrass
  an advisor if a client saw it over their shoulder.
- **Unhurried** — competence is calm. No urgency theatre, no red badges competing for
  attention, no animation that implies the software is excited. Alarm is reserved for things
  that genuinely cannot wait, so that it means something when it appears.

Emotional target: **the confidence of a well-prepared professional.** The advisor should
feel more prepared than they were, not more impressed by the software.

### Aesthetic Direction

**The private-bank brief.** The reference object is the printed client report a good firm
sends quarterly — the artifact this audience already trusts. Strong typographic hierarchy,
hairline rules instead of boxes, generous margins, tabular figures that align down a column,
serif for statements of fact and a civic sans for the working interface.

Concretely:
- **Rules, not cards.** Separate with a hairline and space. Reach for a border only when a
  region genuinely needs enclosing. Never a card inside a card.
- **Figures are typeset, not decorated.** Tabular numerals everywhere a number can be
  compared. Currency aligns on the decimal. No number is ever a "stat tile."
- **Provenance sits with the number**, in smaller type directly beneath it — the way a
  footnote sits under a table in a printed report.
- **Withheld beats wrong.** When a value is unavailable, the space says so plainly and
  names the reason. An em dash and a reason, never a zero and never a blank.

**Anti-references**: the SaaS admin dashboard (hero metric grids, stat tiles, card grids,
"Welcome back" landing pages, gradient nav pills); the "cyber AI" look (neon on black,
glowing accents); and consumer fintech (confetti, streaks, big friendly rounded everything).
An advisor showing any of those to a client looks unserious.

**Theme**: ships **light** (`corporate`) — advisors demo on shared screens, in daylit
offices, and finance documents read as light. `business` is the dark counterpart and must be
equally correct. **Theme-agnostic is a hard constraint**: DaisyUI semantic tokens only, never
a hard-coded color, never an assumption about light or dark, never a hue carrying meaning on
its own. Meaning comes from shape, weight, position, and label.

### Typography

- **Spectral** (serif) for statements of fact: figures an advisor reads aloud, section
  headings, client names, the brief. Chosen because it is engineered for screen text and
  reads as documentary rather than editorial.
- **Public Sans** (sans) for the working interface: labels, controls, navigation, tables.
  The US Web Design System face — institutional and unshowy.
- Both **self-hosted**. A product whose entire claim is that nothing leaves the machine must
  not fetch fonts from a CDN on every page load.
- **No monospace as personality.** Monospace only where character alignment is the point.

### Design Principles

1. **The number is the subject.** Chrome recedes so figures, their provenance, and their
   caveats lead. When in doubt, remove a border rather than add one.

2. **Withheld is a first-class state.** "Could not compute, and here is why" must be as
   designed as a successful figure — not an error, not an empty cell. The product's
   credibility lives in this state.

3. **Theme-agnostic by construction.** DaisyUI semantic tokens only. If a design only works
   in light mode, it is not finished.

4. **Alarm is scarce.** Reserve emphatic treatment for what genuinely cannot wait. If
   everything is flagged, the exclusion-list breach stops being visible.

5. **Earn every pixel.** Before adding a panel, badge, or icon, ask whether it moves the
   advisor closer to being prepared. Density is fine; ornament is not.

6. **Say it once.** No redundant headers, no copy restating a heading, no tutorial text on a
   return visit. Fewer words, more signal.
